RSCM · Readiness–Sovereignty Capability Model

Who controls post-quantum cryptography

Cryptographic sovereignty is not one thing. This explorer decomposes it into three independent constructs, indigenous cryptographic capacity (the general, classical-inclusive base), indigenous post-quantum control (the quantum-safe layer only), and external dependency, so a country’s classical strength is never mistaken for control of the post-quantum layer. A country counts as a post-quantum maker only by clearing a strict gate of demonstrated, institutionally sustained post-quantum capability.

Reading the model. Scores are ordinal levels (0–4) coded from cited public evidence; class depends on the maker gate and the ordering of a single creation level, not on arithmetic across the scale. The maker gate requires level 3 on at least one creation layer (primitive design, implementation, or validation) on a sustained institutional basis; classical capability and governance authority alone never qualify. Retention is the fraction of a 4000-draw plausible-state bootstrap over per-cell evidence grades that keeps a country’s maker verdict. Evidence strength is graded H / M / L per cell. This is a companion to the RSCM paper and reflects a purposive sample of documented cryptographic actors, not a global census.
